# C12 Contract — Curvature Is Not One Matrix

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C12
**Act:** Act II — Dynamics of optimization
**Working title:** Curvature Is Not One Matrix
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can name the
curvature operator behind a “second-order” claim, derive when the realized
Hessian, generalized Gauss–Newton matrix, model Fisher, and empirical Fisher
agree or differ, and probe a chosen symmetric operator without materializing
it.

**Thesis:** Curvature is not a property an implementation simply “has.” It is
an operator selected by the objective, model split, label distribution, and
approximation policy. Choosing or measuring the wrong one can reverse the
diagnosis even on one Bernoulli observation.

## Opening phenomenon

For Bernoulli negative log likelihood with composed logit
(z(w)=w^2), observed label (y=1), and (w=1/2), the current probability is
(p=sigma(1/4)). Along the single parameter,

- realized Hessian: (-0.6295129155);
- generalized Gauss–Newton: (0.2461340827);
- model Fisher: (0.2461340827);
- empirical Fisher: (0.1916894164).

The same loss produces one negative curvature and three nonnegative objects;
two of the latter agree for a declared reason and the fourth does not.

**Prediction:** Which term makes the Hessian negative, why does the model
Fisher equal GGN here, and what distribution would have to change to turn the
empirical Fisher into a genuine estimate of the model Fisher?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c12-curvature-choice`
**Hardware class:** (a), deterministic laptop-CPU mathematics. No timing claim.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** “Use curvature” leaves the matrix unidentified.
2. **Instrumentation:** Hold data, parameters, loss, and arithmetic fixed;
   print the model Jacobian, output-loss curvature, model-curvature residual,
   and four named operators.
3. **Diagnosis:** The Hessian contains loss-through-output curvature plus
   curvature of the parameterized model. GGN drops the second term; the model
   Fisher averages score outer products under model-generated labels; the
   empirical Fisher uses the observed labels.
4. **Theory:** Prove the composite Hessian decomposition, the score/Fisher
   identity under regularity, the compatible-likelihood GGN equality, and the
   Hessian-vector product identity.
5. **Solution:** Require a curvature card: operator, model split, averaging
   distribution, definiteness, approximation structure, product interface,
   damping, state bytes, and interpretation boundary.
6. **Audit:** Diagonal/block/Kronecker structure is an approximation policy,
   not a new truth; an HVP is an access pattern, not an operator name; an
   algebraically identical CG iterate is not automatically a calibrated
   belief.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c12-composite-hessian`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c12-curvature-choice`
- **Assumptions:** twice differentiable (f_\theta\in\mathbb R^q) and loss
  (ell(y,z)), with Jacobian (J), output Hessian (H_z\ell), and component
  Hessians (H_\theta f_j).
- **Conclusion:**
  (H_\theta\ell=J^\mathsf T H_z\ell J+
  \sum_j(\partial\ell/\partial z_j)H_\theta f_j).
- **Proof mode:** full coordinate proof.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** GGN is the first term; it is PSD when the output
  loss is convex, while the omitted model-curvature term can make the realized
  Hessian indefinite.
- **Boundary:** the split between model and output loss is declared and can
  change the GGN.
- **Source:** chain rule; Martens (2020) for the optimization interpretation.

### `thm-c12-fisher-identity`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c12-curvature-choice`
- **Assumptions:** differentiable normalized conditional model
  (p_\theta(y\mid x)), parameter-independent support, and interchange of
  derivative and integral/sum. For the GGN equality, the output variable is a
  compatible natural parameter of the negative-log-likelihood model.
- **Conclusion:** the model score has zero expectation and
  (mathbb E[s_\theta s_\theta^\mathsf T]=
  -\mathbb E[H_\theta\log p_\theta]); under the compatible output split, the
  model Fisher equals GGN.
- **Proof mode:** full score-identity proof; proof-with-pointer for the general
  compatible-output statement.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** replacing model-generated labels by observed labels
  changes the expectation and creates the empirical Fisher, not a free Monte
  Carlo estimate of the same object.
- **Boundary:** misspecification, parameter-dependent support, or a different
  model/loss split can break the stated equalities.
- **Source:** Martens (2020); Kunstner, Balles, and Hennig (2019).

### `thm-c12-hvp`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c12-curvature-choice`
- **Assumptions:** twice differentiable scalar objective and a fixed direction
  (v) independent of the differentiation variable.
- **Conclusion:**
  (H(\theta)v=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\nabla L(\theta+\epsilon v)
  \right|_{\epsilon=0}=\nabla_\theta(\nabla L(\theta)^\mathsf Tv)).
- **Proof mode:** full directional-derivative proof.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** a derivative program can expose Hessian action
  without allocating a dense (p\times p) matrix.
- **Boundary:** the identity names the Hessian only; GGN/Fisher products need
  their own operator program, and algebraic cost is not measured hardware time.
- **Source:** Pearlmutter (1994).
