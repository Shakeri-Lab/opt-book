# C07 Contract — The Sphere Has a Finite Price

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C07
**Act:** Act I — Geometry of high dimensions
**Working title:** The Sphere Has a Finite Price
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can turn a
fixed-point concentration statement into a uniform diagnostic by declaring a
metric, an epsilon-net, its covering cost, and the deterministic regularity
that fills the gaps between anchors.

**Thesis:** A continuum becomes statistically affordable when a finite cover
pays for the sampled anchors and a deterministic interpolation inequality pays
for everything between them.

## Opening phenomenon

A two-dimensional linear map has one hidden direction with stretch six and an
orthogonal direction with stretch one. A small set of predeclared probes can
miss the most stretched direction. A circle epsilon-net still misses it
slightly, but its covering radius converts the largest observed stretch into a
valid upper certificate for the true operator norm.

**Prediction:** Why is a union bound over many probes not yet a statement about
every direction, and what deterministic property can close that gap?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c07-finite-price`
**Hardware class:** (a), laptop-CPU reproducible.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** C05 and C06 control one fixed query, while an operator norm or
   adversarial direction is chosen after the random object is observed.
2. **Instrumentation:** Separate anchor error, covering cost, and
   interpolation error.
3. **Diagnosis:** A finite union bound controls only the declared anchors; a
   continuum needs both a finite cover and regularity between anchors.
4. **Theory:** Define epsilon-nets and covering numbers, prove a volumetric
   upper bound, prove operator-net interpolation, and state a generic
   uniformization theorem for Lipschitz random functions.
5. **Solution:** Budget the log covering number against the fixed-point tail
   exponent and report the residual interpolation term.
6. **Audit:** A weak tail law can be union-bounded formally yet demand a
   useless threshold; discretization does not manufacture concentration.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c07-covering-upper`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c07-finite-price`
- **Assumptions:** Euclidean metric in finite-dimensional real space and
  epsilon greater than zero.
- **Conclusion:** the unit Euclidean ball has an epsilon-net of size at most
  \((1+2/\epsilon)^d\); the same upper bound holds for the unit sphere.
- **Proof mode:** full volumetric proof from a maximal separated set.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** the statistical price is additive as
  \(d\log(1+2/\epsilon)\) in a log-tail budget.
- **Boundary:** the bound is an upper certificate, not an exact covering
  number, and compactness here uses finite-dimensional closedness and
  boundedness.
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c07-operator-net`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c07-finite-price`
- **Assumptions:** a real linear map and an epsilon-net of the unit sphere with
  \(0<\epsilon<1\).
- **Conclusion:** the operator norm is at most
  \((1-\epsilon)^{-1}\) times the largest stretch on the net.
- **Proof mode:** full deterministic proof.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** a sampled maximum becomes a certificate only
  after paying the interpolation factor.
- **Boundary:** the theorem certifies the declared norm and metric only; a
  cloud of unverified random probes is not automatically a net.
- **Source:** standard epsilon-net argument, cross-checked against Vershynin.

### `thm-c07-uniformization`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c07-finite-price`
- **Assumptions:** a compact metric set with a finite epsilon-net, a
  deterministic \(L\)-Lipschitz sample path, and a common fixed-anchor
  sub-Gaussian upper-tail bound.
- **Conclusion:** with failure probability at most delta, the supremum is at
  most the fixed-anchor center plus
  \(\sigma\sqrt{2(\log(2/\delta)+\log|\mathcal N_\epsilon|)}
  +L\epsilon\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof by a finite union bound and deterministic
  interpolation.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** tail exponent, metric entropy, and regularity are
  three separate entries in a uniform guarantee.
- **Boundary:** random or merely local Lipschitz constants require a separate
  event and budget; the theorem does not prove its own fixed-point tail.
- **Source:** finite-cover method synthesized from Vershynin.
