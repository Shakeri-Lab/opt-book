# C10 Contract — Full Rank, Few Directions

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C10
**Act:** Act I — Geometry of high dimensions
**Working title:** Full Rank, Few Directions
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can replace ambient
dimension or algebraic rank with a declared effective/stable-rank diagnostic,
predict when population covariance is estimable from comparatively few samples,
and refuse the stronger claim that one spectral scalar explains generalization
or compressibility.

**Thesis:** Algebraic rank counts nonzero directions, but covariance estimation
is governed by how spectral mass is distributed relative to the strongest
direction. Effective rank measures that ratio; it is a diagnostic complexity,
not a causal theory of learning.

## Opening phenomenon

Construct two (96\times96) Gaussian population covariances. Both have
algebraic rank (96) and operator norm one. One is isotropic and has effective
rank (96); the other has eigenvalues (0.82^j) and effective rank about
(5.56). With (64) matched observations, their median relative
operator-norm covariance errors are about (3.71) and (0.418), respectively.

**Prediction:** If ambient dimension, algebraic rank, and strongest variance are
identical, what remaining spectral quantity predicts the different sample
budgets? Does the smaller number prove that a learned model will generalize?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c10-full-rank-few-directions`
**Hardware class:** (a), laptop-CPU reproducible.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** Dimension and algebraic rank give the same verdict for two
   covariance problems whose finite estimation errors differ sharply.
2. **Instrumentation:** Declare the population spectrum, centering and
   denominator, compute trace, operator norm, algebraic rank, effective rank,
   and matched operator-error distributions.
3. **Diagnosis:** The ambient rate in C09 replaced
   \(\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\) by the crude ceiling
   \(d\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\); the lost ratio is effective rank.
4. **Theory:** Prove the effective-rank bounds and stable-rank identity, compute
   the Gaussian rank-one variance proxy, and state the sub-Gaussian
   effective-rank covariance theorem with a proof pointer.
5. **Solution:** Report a spectral-capacity card containing object, spectrum,
   normalization, effective/stable rank, approximation tail when relevant, and
   the theorem whose assumptions make the number operational.
6. **Audit:** Two spectra can share one effective rank while having different
   tails; covariance theorems do not automatically apply to weights, updates,
   Hessians, or generalization; a learned spike can increase trace as well as
   the upper edge.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c10-effective-rank`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c10-full-rank-few-directions`
- **Assumptions:** a nonzero (d\times d) symmetric PSD matrix
  \(\Sigma\) with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0\).
- **Conclusion:**
  \(r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)/
  \|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sum_i\lambda_i/\lambda_1\) and
  \(1\le r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Sigma)\le\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma)\le d\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof from the eigenvalues.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** a full-rank matrix can have effective rank
  arbitrarily close to one.
- **Boundary:** the scalar does not locate a truncation threshold or encode the
  full spectral tail.
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c10-stable-rank`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c10-full-rank-few-directions`
- **Assumptions:** a nonzero real matrix (A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\).
- **Conclusion:**
  \(\operatorname{sr}(A)=\|A\|_{\mathrm F}^2/
  \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2=r_{\mathrm{eff}}(A^{\mathsf T}A)
  =r_{\mathrm{eff}}(AA^{\mathsf T})\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof from singular values.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** stable rank is the appropriate soft count for a
  rectangular map; effective rank applies to its PSD Gram operator.
- **Boundary:** for a PSD matrix (\Sigma\), \(\operatorname{sr}(\Sigma)\)
  generally differs from (r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Sigma)\).
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c10-gaussian-variance`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c10-full-rank-few-directions`
- **Assumptions:** (X\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\) with PSD covariance and
  (Y=XX^{\mathsf T}-\Sigma\).
- **Conclusion:**
  \(\mathbb E Y^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\Sigma+\Sigma^2\), so
  \(\|\mathbb E Y^2\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le
  (r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Sigma)+1)\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof by the Gaussian fourth-moment identity.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** trace mass enters the matrix variance proxy before
  any ambient-dimension upper bound is imposed.
- **Boundary:** Gaussian fourth moments do not establish the full theorem for
  arbitrary sub-Gaussian or heavy-tailed vectors.
- **Source:** Gaussian moment calculation; cross-checked against effective-rank
  covariance proofs.

### `thm-c10-covariance-error`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c10-full-rank-few-directions`
- **Assumptions:** independent copies of a centered vector satisfying
  \(\|\langle X,x\rangle\|_{\psi_2}\le
  K\|\langle X,x\rangle\|_{L^2}\) for every (x), with covariance
  \(\Sigma\).
- **Conclusion:** for (m) samples,
  \(\mathbb E\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le
  CK^4(\sqrt{r/m}+r/m)\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), where
  (r=r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Sigma)\).
- **Proof mode:** proof-with-pointer; the chapter exposes the Gaussian variance
  mechanism and points to the matrix-deviation argument.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** relative error (\varepsilon\) needs a sample budget
  on the order of (r/\varepsilon^2\), up to constants and the declared tail
  parameter, rather than automatically (d/\varepsilon^2\).
- **Boundary:** expectation is not a finite confidence threshold, and the
  theorem is not distribution-free.
- **Source:** Vershynin; Koltchinskii and Lounici for Gaussian covariance
  operators.
