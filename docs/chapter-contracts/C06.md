# C06 Contract — One Square, Two Regimes

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C06
**Act:** Act I — Geometry of high dimensions
**Working title:** One Square, Two Regimes
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can recognize when a
routine square or product has left the sub-Gaussian safe zone because they can
inspect the MGF domain, select a sub-exponential scale, read Bernstein's two
regimes, and audit the bias introduced by bounding a heavy-tailed quantity.

**Thesis:** Squaring a sub-Gaussian variable preserves exponential
concentration but destroys global quadratic MGF control; a local MGF creates
Bernstein's quadratic-to-linear transition, while variables without a positive
MGF require a changed estimator rather than a stronger Chernoff calculation.

## Opening phenomenon

Let \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\) and \(X=Z^2-1\). The input is sub-Gaussian and
\(\var(X)=2\), but

\[
\log \E e^{\lambda X}
=-\lambda-\frac12\log(1-2\lambda)
\]

exists only for \(\lambda<1/2\). A variance-matched quadratic tail candidate
\(\exp(-t^2/4)\) first fails on the chapter's declared grid at \(t=4\):
the exact upper-tail probability is about \(0.02535\), whereas the candidate
is about \(0.01832\). The finite MGF boundary, not this one grid point, proves
that no global sub-Gaussian proxy exists.

**Prediction:** Does centering undo the damage caused by squaring? If not,
which part of the C05 Chernoff optimization becomes unavailable?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c06-square-two-regimes`
**Hardware class:** (a), laptop-CPU reproducible.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** Norms, energies, covariance entries, and curvature proxies
   square or multiply quantities that were individually sub-Gaussian.
2. **Instrumentation:** Inspect the exact log-MGF domain and upper-tail rate,
   not variance alone.
3. **Diagnosis:** The MGF remains quadratic only near the origin and ceases to
   exist beyond a finite parameter; the free Chernoff optimizer is now
   constrained.
4. **Theory:** Introduce the \(\psi_1\) Orlicz norm, prove that squaring maps
   \(\psi_2\) control to \(\psi_1\) control, and derive Bernstein's
   quadratic/linear bound for independent centered sums.
5. **Solution:** Use a sub-exponential scale and two-regime threshold when the
   MGF is local; if no positive MGF exists, change the estimator through an
   explicitly bounded transform or a later robust procedure.
6. **Audit:** Bounding or clipping creates concentration for the transformed
   variable but may bias the original target, and the bounding norm itself
   must be computed without overflowing.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c06-square-subexponential`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c06-square-two-regimes`
- **Assumptions:** real \(X,Y\) with finite \(\psi_2\) norms; no independence is
  required for the product statement.
- **Conclusion:**
  \(\norm{X^2}_{\psi_1}=\norm{X}_{\psi_2}^2\), and centering \(X^2\)
  changes this scale by at most a universal constant factor; moreover,
  \(\norm{XY}_{\psi_1}\le\norm{X}_{\psi_2}\norm{Y}_{\psi_2}\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof from the Orlicz definitions.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** squared norms and diagonal covariance terms need a
  \(\psi_1\), not a \(\psi_2\), contract.
- **Boundary:** the two-factor product result permits dependence, but repeated
  products and arbitrary nonlinear transforms require separate analysis.
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c06-bernstein`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c06-square-two-regimes`
- **Assumptions:** independent centered sub-exponential variables with declared
  \(K_i=\norm{X_i}_{\psi_1}\).
- **Conclusion:** for all \(t\ge0\),
  \[
  \Pr\!\left(\left|\sum_i X_i\right|\ge t\right)
  \le
  2\exp\!\left[
  -c\min\!\left(
  \frac{t^2}{\sum_i K_i^2},
  \frac{t}{\max_i K_i}
  \right)
  \right].
  \]
- **Proof mode:** full constrained-Chernoff proof after the local MGF lemma.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** moderate deviations use the unconstrained
  quadratic optimizer; large deviations hit the MGF boundary and pay a linear
  exponent.
- **Boundary:** independence is needed for factorization; unspecified constants
  do not authorize a numerical threshold without a distribution-specific
  calibration.
- **Source:** Vershynin and Bernstein's exponential-moment method.

### `thm-c06-clipping-contract`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c06-square-two-regimes`
- **Assumptions:** integrable scalar \(X\), symmetric clipping
  \(T_C(X)\in[-C,C]\), and centering by \(\E T_C(X)\).
- **Conclusion:** \(T_C(X)-\E T_C(X)\) is sub-Gaussian with MGF proxy variance
  at most \(C^2\); the original-target bias is
  \(\E T_C(X)-\E X\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof from Hoeffding's lemma plus the definition of
  bias.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** clipping repairs the transformed variable's tail
  class only by changing the estimator.
- **Boundary:** the theorem does not choose \(C\), preserve the original mean,
  or make an overflowed norm computation finite.
- **Source:** Hoeffding; clipping-bias bridge cross-checked against Koloskova,
  Hendrikx, and Stich.
