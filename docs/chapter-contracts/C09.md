# C09 Contract — The Bulk Is Not a Verdict

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C09
**Act:** Act I — Geometry of high dimensions
**Working title:** The Bulk Is Not a Verdict
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can turn a sample
covariance spectrum into an empirical spectral distribution, state the
Marchenko--Pastur baseline under a consistent scaling convention, and decide
whether an apparent upper-edge outlier survives a matched finite-size null and
a declared alternative model.

**Thesis:** Marchenko--Pastur describes the asymptotic spectral mass generated
by a particular null model. Its support edge is not, by itself, a finite-sample
significance threshold; calling an outlier signal requires a statistic, a
finite null, a false-positive budget, and an alternative whose separation
conditions are stated.

## Opening phenomenon

Generate a zero-mean Gaussian data matrix with \(m=128\) observations and
\(n=64\) coordinates, and form
\(\widehat{\Sigma}=X^{\mathsf T}X/m\). In a seeded pure-noise realization, the
largest eigenvalue lies above the asymptotic Marchenko--Pastur upper edge.
Across an independently seeded finite null ensemble of the same shape, such
crossings are routine and the observed value remains below the predeclared
95th-percentile null threshold.

**Prediction:** Does crossing the asymptotic edge prove that the realization
contains a population spike? Which finite-sample quantity is needed before the
word “signal” is admissible?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
**Hardware class:** (a), laptop-CPU reproducible.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** A pure-noise finite covariance matrix can place its largest
   eigenvalue above the limiting support edge.
2. **Instrumentation:** Declare the sample orientation and denominator,
   compute the empirical spectral distribution, compare it with the
   Marchenko--Pastur density, then calibrate the chosen extreme-eigenvalue
   statistic under a matched finite null.
3. **Diagnosis:** The empirical spectral distribution is a finite measure;
   Marchenko--Pastur is an asymptotic bulk law; an edge crossing is a test
   statistic only after a finite rejection rule is supplied.
4. **Theory:** Prove the trace identities for empirical spectral moments,
   state Marchenko--Pastur and covariance-estimation guarantees with proof
   pointers, prove the repeated-look false-alarm calculation, and state the
   rank-one spiked-covariance separation theorem with a proof pointer.
5. **Solution:** Use a spectral-null contract: null model, estimator, matrix
   shape, centering and denominator, statistic, finite calibration method,
   false-positive level, number of looks, and alternative model.
6. **Audit:** A bulk fit does not validate independence or isotropy; an
   outlier is not automatically learned signal; a learned parameter matrix or
   Hessian does not inherit a Marchenko--Pastur null without a justified
   generative model.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c09-esd-moments`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
- **Assumptions:** a real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix with eigenvalues
  \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\).
- **Conclusion:** its empirical spectral distribution
  \(\mu_M=n^{-1}\sum_j\delta_{\lambda_j}\) is a probability measure and
  \(\int \lambda^k\,d\mu_M=n^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(M^k)\) for every
  nonnegative integer \(k\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof by orthogonal diagonalization.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** the mean eigenvalue is normalized trace, while
  an edge statistic is a different functional of the same finite measure.
- **Boundary:** finitely many moments or a histogram do not uniquely validate
  a generative null in a finite study.
- **Source:** standard spectral theorem; derivation order follows the
  instructor's canonical RMT material.

### `thm-c09-marchenko-pastur`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
- **Assumptions:** \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\) has independent entries
  with mean zero, variance one, and a suitable finite-moment condition, while
  \(n/m\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)\).
- **Conclusion:** the empirical spectral distribution of
  \(X^{\mathsf T}X/m\) converges to the Marchenko--Pastur law with continuous
  density on
  \([(1-\sqrt{\gamma})^2,(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^2]\) and a zero atom of mass
  \((1-1/\gamma)_+\).
- **Proof mode:** proof-with-pointer; the resolvent or moment machinery is not
  reproved in full.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** aspect ratio predicts an asymptotic null bulk for
  the declared scaling and exposes rank deficiency when \(n>m\).
- **Boundary:** weak convergence of the empirical measure is not an exact
  finite-matrix confinement theorem or a calibrated test of the largest
  eigenvalue.
- **Source:** Marchenko and Pastur (1967), cross-checked against modern
  sample-covariance statements.

### `thm-c09-covariance-error`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
- **Assumptions:** independent copies of a centered sub-Gaussian vector in
  \(\mathbb{R}^n\), with covariance \(\Sigma\) and a relative
  \(\psi_2\)-to-\(L^2\) constant \(K\).
- **Conclusion:** the expected operator-norm error of the sample covariance is
  at most
  \(CK^2(\sqrt{n/m}+n/m)\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).
- **Proof mode:** diagnostic proof sketch by whitening and invoking C08's
  random-operator bound; pointer for constant bookkeeping.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** when \(m\) and \(n\) are comparable, the sample
  covariance need not be close to the population covariance in operator norm,
  even though its empirical spectrum has a deterministic bulk shape.
- **Boundary:** the ambient-dimension rate is not the final complexity
  measure; C10 owns the effective-rank refinement.
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c09-repeated-looks`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
- **Assumptions:** \(K\) inspected spectra and a per-look event with null
  probability at most \(\alpha\).
- **Conclusion:** the probability of at least one false alarm is at most
  \(K\alpha\); when the looks are independent with exact probability
  \(\alpha\), it is \(1-(1-\alpha)^K\).
- **Proof mode:** full proof by the union bound and the probability of the
  complementary independent event.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** monitoring more layers, checkpoints, seeds, or
  candidate matrices consumes a larger false-positive budget.
- **Boundary:** dependence preserves the union bound but invalidates the exact
  independent-look formula.
- **Source:** elementary probability.

### `thm-c09-spike-separation`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c09-bulk-not-verdict`
- **Assumptions:** a rank-one spiked population covariance with leading
  eigenvalue \(\beta>1\), unit remaining eigenvalues, independent standardized
  entries with finite fourth moment, and \(n/m\to\gamma\in(0,1)\).
- **Conclusion:** if \(\beta\le1+\sqrt{\gamma}\), the largest sample eigenvalue
  converges to \((1+\sqrt{\gamma})^2\); if
  \(\beta>1+\sqrt{\gamma}\), it separates and converges to
  \(\beta(1+\gamma/(\beta-1))\).
- **Proof mode:** proof-with-pointer.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** “signal” is a claim relative to an alternative
  model, and even a real population spike can be absorbed by the null bulk
  below the separation threshold.
- **Boundary:** the result is asymptotic and model-specific; it does not turn
  arbitrary trained spectra into spiked Wishart models.
- **Source:** Baik and Silverstein (2006).
