# C08 Contract — One Average, Two Edges

## Identity

**Unit ID:** C08
**Act:** Act I — Geometry of high dimensions
**Working title:** One Average, Two Edges
**Student transformation:** After this chapter, the reader can distinguish
average norm preservation from uniform conditioning by inspecting singular
values, aspect ratio, and the difference between fixed and data-dependent
directions.

**Thesis:** Isotropy centers the squared stretch of every fixed direction, but
the singular edges describe the directions selected after the matrix is seen;
their separation is a geometric quantity, not an arithmetic failure by itself.

## Opening phenomenon

For one square Gaussian matrix scaled by the reciprocal square root of its row
count, a fixed predeclared unit vector has stretch near one. The top and bottom
right singular vectors of that same realized matrix have very different
stretches. The average is correct; the adaptive extremes are not represented
by it.

**Prediction:** Which claim does isotropy justify: preservation of one fixed
direction in expectation, or simultaneous preservation of every direction?

**Phenomenon ID:** `c08-one-average-two-edges`
**Hardware class:** (a), laptop-CPU reproducible.

## Causal chain

1. **Problem:** An average or fixed-direction check can approve a matrix whose
   realized action has widely separated extremal directions.
2. **Instrumentation:** Compute the SVD control, compare fixed and singular
   directions, record Frobenius and operator summaries, and use power iteration
   only as an upper-edge estimator.
3. **Diagnosis:** Isotropy is a fixed-direction population statement; singular
   vectors are data-dependent extrema.
4. **Theory:** Prove the SVD stretch interval, connect the C07 net argument to
   a nonasymptotic sub-Gaussian matrix bound, and state the sharp Gaussian edge
   benchmark with a proof pointer.
5. **Solution:** Treat aspect ratio and both singular edges as part of the
   matrix diagnostic; validate approximate instruments against an exact small
   control.
6. **Audit:** Controlling the largest singular value says nothing by itself
   about the smallest; a small singular value is not synonymous with
   floating-point underflow.

## Theory and theorem ledger

### `thm-c08-svd-stretch`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c08-one-average-two-edges`
- **Assumptions:** a real \(m\times n\) matrix with \(m\ge n\).
- **Conclusion:** every vector satisfies
  \(\sigma_{\min}(A)\|x\|_2\le\|Ax\|_2
  \le\sigma_{\max}(A)\|x\|_2\), with equality at the corresponding
  right singular vectors.
- **Proof mode:** full proof from the SVD.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** a uniform near-isometry requires both edges, not
  an average squared stretch or the upper edge alone.
- **Boundary:** rank-deficient and wide matrices have a zero lower edge on
  their full input space.
- **Source:** standard singular-value decomposition, cross-checked against
  Vershynin.

### `thm-c08-subgaussian-edges`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c08-one-average-two-edges`
- **Assumptions:** independent, centered, isotropic, sub-Gaussian rows with a
  common declared row scale.
- **Conclusion:** with high probability, all singular values lie between
  \(\sqrt m-CK^2(\sqrt n+t)\) and
  \(\sqrt m+CK^2(\sqrt n+t)\).
- **Proof mode:** diagnostic proof sketch showing fixed-direction
  concentration, C07's net, and C06's squared-variable Bernstein step;
  pointer for constant bookkeeping.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** the center comes from isotropy and the width pays
  for an adaptive sphere of directions.
- **Boundary:** the universal constant is not a calibrated numerical
  confidence interval, and the lower statement is informative only when its
  right-hand side is positive.
- **Source:** Vershynin.

### `thm-c08-gaussian-edges`

- **Phenomenon explained:** `c08-one-average-two-edges`
- **Assumptions:** an \(m\times n\) matrix with independent standard-normal
  entries and \(m\ge n\).
- **Conclusion:** the expected upper and lower edges are bounded by
  \(\sqrt m+\sqrt n\) and \(\sqrt m-\sqrt n\), respectively, and Gaussian
  concentration supplies sub-Gaussian deviations around the corresponding
  expectations.
- **Proof mode:** proof-with-pointer to Gaussian comparison and concentration.
- **Diagnostic corollary:** after scaling by \(1/\sqrt m\), aspect ratio
  predicts the benchmark interval \(1\pm\sqrt{n/m}\).
- **Boundary:** the square lower benchmark is zero and hence provides no
  positive conditioning guarantee; it is not an underflow theorem.
- **Source:** Gordon's Gaussian comparison method as presented by Vershynin.
